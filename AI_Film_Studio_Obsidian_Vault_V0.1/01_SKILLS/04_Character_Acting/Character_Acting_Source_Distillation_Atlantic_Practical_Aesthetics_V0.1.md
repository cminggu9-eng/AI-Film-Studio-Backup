## Source boundary

Only the official PDF pp. 13–16 was used. It defines Literal, Want, Action, As-If, tactics/tools, and living in the moment. The output is a high-fidelity capability translation, not a claim about Atlantic's complete pedagogy or a screen-directing rule.

| CA-D | Source-supported reusable method | CA-C mapping | Screen transfer | Boundary |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| CA-D01 | Start by separating what the text literally has the character doing from interpretation. | C01, C02 | `DIRECT` | Use supplied Canon/assignment facts only; do not invent biography or knowledge. |
| CA-D02 | Identify the immediate want directed toward the other person in this scene/beat. | C03, C13 | `DIRECT` | It is a playable scene-local objective, not a Showrunner arc rewrite. |
| CA-D03 | Choose an action as an active pursuit of that want, expressed as what the performer attempts with the partner. | C04, C06, C08 | `DIRECT` | Do not prescribe a facial result, dialogue rewrite, or guaranteed partner response. |
| CA-D04 | Use a personal As-If only as private preparation to make the chosen action urgent and specific. | C07, C15, C17 | `CONDITIONAL` | Never expose it as invented character fact, Canon, or a required personal disclosure. |
| CA-D05 | Let tactics vary in response to the partner while retaining the chosen action; live response is not pre-choreographed reaction. | C05, C12, C14, C20 | `DIRECT` | No director blocking/camera control and no mandatory tactic change on every line. |
