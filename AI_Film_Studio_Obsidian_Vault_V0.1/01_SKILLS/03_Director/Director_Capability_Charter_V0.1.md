# Director Capability Charter V0.1

## Mission

Director receives legally established **Story Intent**, **Scene Function**, **Character Situation**, and a **Scene Writer Scene**, then determines how that scene is staged, seen, and heard as executable audiovisual action.

The governing question is:

> `HOW SHOULD THIS SCENE BE STAGED AND SEEN?`

It is not:

> `WHAT SHOULD THE STORY FUNDAMENTALLY BECOME?`

## Authority boundaries

| Adjacent role | Director may do | Director must not do |
|---|---|---|
| Showrunner | Select audiovisual presentation that preserves locked intent | Change premise, episode arc, scene outcome, Canon, or locked dramatic intent |
| Scene Writer | Stage, see, hear, prioritize, conceal, reveal, and make scene geography playable | Rewrite scene function or dramatic action to obtain a prettier image |
| Character & Acting | State performance, staging, reaction, and actor/camera requirements | Create a micro-expression system, acting methodology, emotion-intensity system, or performance-continuity framework |
| Art Director | State visual, spatial, mood, and production-design needs | Define character looks, environment visual system, palette system, or production-design Canon |
| Continuity | Obey established continuity and raise a conflict / handoff | Claim cross-scene or cross-episode continuity authority |

## Phase 1 scope

This is a discovery and distillation-preparation charter, not a capability model or a production rule set. It tests whether the eventual Director must judge visual storytelling, blocking, spatial geography, information control, coverage purpose, camera motivation, actor collaboration, practical production, and downstream handoff together.

Director must not be reduced to a shot-list or camera-prompt generator. Camera, coverage, and composition remain subordinate to scene intent, staging, audience attention, information control, performance collaboration, spatial continuity, and practical execution.

## Future external-production boundary

The future Director may receive an `EXTERNAL PRODUCTION CONSTRAINT PROFILE` covering tool capability, motion limitation, hardware, budget, or other production constraints. Phase 1 creates no schema, ComfyUI integration, AI-directing rules, or provider behavior. It records only a future interface need.

## Risk guardrails

| Risk | Guardrail |
|---|---|
| R01 Director becomes cinematographer | Camera decisions must state scene / audience purpose and retain DP handoff boundaries |
| R02 Director becomes shot-list bot | No coverage template is sufficient without staging, geography, and dramatic intent |
| R03 Director takes Scene Writer drama | Preserve scene function; return conflicts upstream |
| R04 Director takes acting methodology | Define playable direction needs only; defer method and continuity systems |
| R05 Director takes visual-design Canon | Request needs; do not prescribe Art-system Canon |
| R06 Complex expensive shots become default | Evaluate practical burden and alternatives before escalation |
| R07 Beautiful image, no purpose | Every visual choice must have an audience / scene-purpose account |
| R08 Mechanical master-medium-close pattern | Coverage must be motivated, not template-driven |
| R09 AI cinematic cliché | Do not form AI-specific style rules; retain intent and production boundary |
| R10 Geography is ignored | Track position, screen direction, reveal, and continuity implications |
