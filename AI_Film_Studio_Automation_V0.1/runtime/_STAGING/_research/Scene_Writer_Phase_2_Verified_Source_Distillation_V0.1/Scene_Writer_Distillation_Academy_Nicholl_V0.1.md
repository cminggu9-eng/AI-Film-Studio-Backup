# Scene Writer Distillation — Academy Nicholl V0.1

## A. Source Scope

**Actually read:** Academy-hosted *Screenwriting: Activities for the Classroom*, viewer PDF pp.3–6: “Structuring a Screenplay”, “Developing a Screenplay from a Book”, and activity masters on visual story, action, dialogue and formatting.

**Not read or transferred:** the Academy resource landing page as a teaching body, the incompletely extractable format sample, competition eligibility rules, the cited novels/films’ dialogue or scenes, personal writer style, cost practice, AI-production rules, and Director-owned shot choices.

**Evidence boundary:** this is an educational guide about screenwriting and visual literacy. Its story-structure material is broad; it cannot by itself establish scene-turn or scene-exit doctrine.

## B. Primary Contributions

- Film is framed as a visual medium in which the viewer can see action unfold.
- In an adaptation exercise, inner thought from prose must be conveyed by the screenplay and performer rather than reported as novel interiority.
- The guide invites examination of what an audience learns from action versus dialogue and notes that a visual story need not rely on much dialogue.
- It differentiates submission/first-draft scripts from shooting scripts, which supports a boundary against premature camera design.

## C. Distilled Methods

### SW-D04 — Viewer-Legible Event Check

- **Capability Name:** Viewer-Legible Event Check
- **Problem It Solves:** A scene describes a story result but does not give the viewer an event through which to register it.
- **Trigger / Detection:** A draft asks “what changed?” but the answer is only an abstract statement, internal conclusion, or hidden plot summary.
- **Decision Principle:** For a film scene, identify the action the viewer can encounter. “Visual” does not mean silent, nor does it require a camera prescription.
- **Action / Transformation:** Ask for or supply the smallest visible/hearable event that carries the approved story information; retain dialogue where it performs an active scene function.
- **Stop Condition:** Stop when the event is legible; do not choose framing, shot order, or actor interpretation.
- **Failure Risk:** Converting every point to pantomime and losing dialogue, sound, or approved narration.
- **Classification:** `Hard Constraint` for a Scene Writer claim that something happens in a film scene.
- **Source Trace:** E-AN-01, viewer PDF p.3, “Structuring a Screenplay”: visual-medium thinking; viewer PDF p.5 / activity master: film is a visual medium and the viewer must be able to see action unfold.

### SW-D05 — Inner-State Externalization Prompt

- **Capability Name:** Inner-State Externalization Prompt
- **Problem It Solves:** A prose adaptation retains a character’s private thought/feeling without any screenplay or performance carrier.
- **Trigger / Detection:** A sentence explains what a character thinks or feels but offers no playable or visible manifestation.
- **Decision Principle:** When converting prose interiority, ask how the writing and performance can make the state legible; do not assert a single correct gesture or use this as a general ban on all interior devices.
- **Action / Transformation:** Identify the stimulus and an externally available response, choice, silence, line, or interaction; preserve uncertainty if the source material leaves it uncertain.
- **Stop Condition:** Stop once an actor/playable carrier is specified at the narrative level, before prescribing the performance.
- **Failure Risk:** Over-directing acting or converting nuanced interiority into a simplistic action label.
- **Classification:** `Conditional Method` — when prose/internal material is being converted for screen.
- **Source Trace:** E-AN-01, viewer PDF p.4, “Developing a Screenplay from a Book”: contrasts prose telling of inner feeling with film’s reliance on writer and actor to show it.

### SW-D06 — Goal–Obstacle Trace, Not Goal Invention

- **Capability Name:** Goal–Obstacle Trace
- **Problem It Solves:** An approved story goal appears in the scene without any resistance that could create visible action.
- **Trigger / Detection:** Showrunner has supplied a character goal, but the proposed scene neither tests it nor indicates what resists it.
- **Decision Principle:** Trace the approved goal to an obstacle, crisis, or conflict that prevents straightforward achievement. This is a check on provided story material, not permission to invent a new plot goal or decide macro structure.
- **Action / Transformation:** Mark the obstacle’s scene-facing form (person, information, environment, timing, consequence); return to Showrunner if adding resistance would alter locked facts.
- **Stop Condition:** Stop if the only viable obstacle changes a locked story outcome or character core.
- **Failure Risk:** Mistaking an act-level educational example for proof that every scene requires a new conflict or that Scene Writer owns the central dramatic question.
- **Classification:** `Conditional Method`.
- **Source Trace:** E-AN-01, viewer PDF pp.3 and 5, “Structuring a Screenplay” / activity master: conflict, crises and obstacles are described as blocking a protagonist’s goal; scope is story-level, hence this method remains conditional.

### SW-D07 — Action–Dialogue Information Allocation

- **Capability Name:** Action–Dialogue Information Allocation
- **Problem It Solves:** Information is placed in dialogue by default even when visible action can carry part of it, or action is used when dialogue must perform a relationship move.
- **Trigger / Detection:** A scene’s information can be received only as a line of explanation, or the draft has not asked what the audience learns from behavior versus speech.
- **Decision Principle:** Test the information carrier: what is learned from action and what is learned from dialogue? A visual story does not need large amounts of dialogue, but the source does not require dialogue removal.
- **Action / Transformation:** Assign each necessary item to action, dialogue, or both according to its dramatic function; do not recast information as meaningless business.
- **Stop Condition:** Stop when the audience can receive the needed information and the characters still have an active relation in the scene.
- **Failure Risk:** Treating “not much dialogue” as a hard rule, or treating action and dialogue as mutually exclusive.
- **Classification:** `Default Heuristic`.
- **Source Trace:** E-AN-01, viewer PDF p.6 / activity master: compares learning from character actions and speech, and notes that film is visual and does not require much dialogue.

### SW-D08 — Draft-Form Authority Boundary

- **Capability Name:** Draft-Form Authority Boundary
- **Problem It Solves:** An early scene draft claims shooting-script authority through scene numbers or extensive camera instructions.
- **Trigger / Detection:** Draft output contains shot design or production markings that do not change the scene’s narrative fact.
- **Decision Principle:** Submission/sales/first-draft scripts differ from shooting scripts; early scene writing ordinarily avoids scene numbers and most camera design.
- **Action / Transformation:** Preserve the action, setting, and dialogue need; route final camera/shot treatment to future Director/Cinematography work.
- **Stop Condition:** Stop after routing; do not erase an explicitly user-approved production-script request.
- **Failure Risk:** Applying a submission-format convention to every kind of production document.
- **Classification:** `Conditional Method` — only for early/spec-style screenplay output.
- **Source Trace:** E-AN-01, viewer PDF p.3, “Formatting the Script”: submission/sales/first-draft scripts are distinct from shooting scripts and use few, if any, camera shots.

## E. Studio Translation

The Academy material becomes an operational distinction: the Scene Writer makes an approved story event observable, determines whether information is better carried by action, dialogue, or both, and checks a supplied goal for a scene-facing obstacle without assuming Showrunner authority. It explicitly preserves future acting and directing ownership.

## F. Non-Transferable Content

- The guide’s classroom exercises, school audience, page-per-minute illustration and three-act teaching simplification.
- Competition or submission policy from the Nicholl web pages, including any AI eligibility rule.
- Exact screenplay formatting and sample-film content.
- Any inference that an act-level plot point proves the structure of every individual scene.
- No source support for a scene turn, scene end point, production-cost estimation, or AI-production constraint.
