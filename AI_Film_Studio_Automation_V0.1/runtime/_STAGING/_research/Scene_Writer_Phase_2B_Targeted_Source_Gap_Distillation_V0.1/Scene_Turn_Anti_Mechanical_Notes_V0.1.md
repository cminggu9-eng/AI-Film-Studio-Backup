# Scene Turn Anti-Mechanical Notes V0.1

> This note records the limits of the Phase 2B Scriptnotes evidence. It does not establish a final Scene Turn taxonomy or Studio-Native Rule.

## Supported evidence

|Evidence|What it supports|What it does not support|
|---|---|---|
|E-SN-01 scene checklist asks what must occur and how the scene changes the direction of the story.|A scene-change inquiry may be used to identify dramatic function.|A fixed list of permitted turn types.|
|E-SN-01 describes characters with goals/ambitions whose own actions cause change in the scene.|Turn/change can be grounded in choice, action, dialogue or reaction rather than a writer-announced beat.|Every scene must have a large reversal, reveal, argument or event.|
|E-SN-01 explicitly warns that a rigid checklist and planned beat optimization can paralyze/discourage discovery; it mentions the danger of every scene being forced through a positive/negative flip.|Do not make turn a mechanical polarity formula.|That scenes need no structure or function.|
|E-SN-02 says a character making a change within a scene can be helpful, while framing it as a general principle rather than an absolute condition.|Specific action can make the change legible.|A hard requirement that every sentence or every scene contain a dramatic reversal.|

## Evidence-backed working boundary

- A **scene turn** may be investigated as a meaningful scene-state or story-direction change connected to what characters do, say, or react to.
- The eligible sources do **not** authorize a mandatory realization, decision, control shift, information shift, relationship shift, stakes shift, or closed category set.
- The eligible sources do **not** authorize `EVERY SCENE MUST TURN BIG`, `EVERY SCENE MUST REVEAL`, `EVERY SCENE MUST ARGUE`, or `EVERY SCENE MUST FLIP POSITIVE/NEGATIVE`.
- In a future authorized Cross-Distillation, this evidence may be compared with other source-specific methods; it cannot be elevated here.
