# Scene Writer Unshootable Language Taxonomy Draft V0.1

> This is a **DRAFT diagnostic taxonomy**, not a keyword blacklist, a final pass/fail gate, or a production capability model. A phrase is weakly shootable only in its actual field and context—especially when it claims a screen event without an observable, hearable, playable, or separately authorised carrier.

## Cross-Distilled Diagnostic Order

1. **Locate the field.** Is this scene action, dialogue, a project-approved narration/voice-over field, concise permitted framing, or a planning note?
2. **Lock meaning.** What fact, uncertainty, relation, motive boundary, or causal strength must not change?
3. **Check carrier.** Can the audience encounter the information through action, choice, reaction, object/sound/spatial change, dialogue, subtext, clue, silence, or an authorised device?
4. **Test scene function.** Does a conversion keep the present pressure and information allocation alive?
5. **Stop before other roles.** Do not turn the fix into a camera plan, acting blueprint, or voice-QA rewrite.

## A. Pure Internal Summary

**Example:** “他意识到事情不对。”

|Question|Draft answer|
|---|---|
|Why it can be weakly shootable|It reports a private conclusion as action without supplying the stimulus or a screen carrier.|
|Lawful conditions|It may be a project-approved narration/voice-over device, a non-action planning note, or a concise form explicitly authorised by the project/format.|
|When to transform|When it occupies scene action and the audience would need direct mental access to know what changed.|
|Conversion channels|Visible stimulus plus changed choice, altered plan, interruption, line, refusal, object interaction, or a changed response.|
|Meaning preservation risk|Replacing “something is wrong” with a specific accusation can overstate knowledge; a generic stare can erase causal specificity.|
|Anti-mechanical warning|Do not ban “realises” as a word. Keep it when the field and authority are legal; otherwise change only enough to supply an appropriate carrier.|

## B. Emotion Label

**Example:** “她感到十分复杂。”

|Question|Draft answer|
|---|---|
|Why it can be weakly shootable|The label asks the viewer to receive a named feeling without knowing its current pressure, action, or relation.|
|Lawful conditions|A permitted narration/interior device, a deliberately concise literary framing authorised by format, or dialogue in which the character names the feeling for an active purpose.|
|When to transform|When it substitutes for the scene-facing choice, hesitation, response, or consequence that the moment requires.|
|Conversion channels|Hesitation, avoidance, competing action, silence, an active line, a changed relationship move, or an object/action with clear current relevance.|
|Meaning preservation risk|Converting complexity into one obvious gesture or one diagnostic emotion flattens ambiguity and pre-empts acting.|
|Anti-mechanical warning|Not every feeling needs a visible “tell”; a silence, a direct line, or an authorised concise description can be the proper carrier.|

## C. Relationship Summary

**Example:** “他们的关系发生了变化。”

|Question|Draft answer|
|---|---|
|Why it can be weakly shootable|It announces an outcome rather than letting the audience encounter the changed access, trust, alliance, distance, power, or obligation.|
|Lawful conditions|A retrospective planning statement, a permitted narration device, or a concise transition note that is not presented as the full dramatic event.|
|When to transform|When the scene action relies on the summary instead of an enacted exchange, decision, refusal, concession, or new behaviour.|
|Conversion channels|Changed address, access granted/withdrawn, request refused/accepted, information shared/withheld, altered commitment, physical/spatial relation necessary to logic, or dialogue action.|
|Meaning preservation risk|A conversion can falsely decide whether the change is reconciliation, rupture, romance, or submission.|
|Anti-mechanical warning|Do not require a confrontation. Relation can change through cooperation, silence, protection, discovery, or a choice.|

## D. Backstory Explanation

**Example:** “他一直因为童年经历而害怕……”

|Question|Draft answer|
|---|---|
|Why it can be weakly shootable|It imports history as an explanatory verdict without checking what the present scene needs the audience to know.|
|Lawful conditions|Approved narration/voice-over, direct dialogue with current relational action, or a relevant clue/detail that gives the audience a usable inference.|
|When to transform|When the explanation dumps total history before it has present dramatic relevance or replaces current action.|
|Conversion channels|A clue/nugget, an active question/answer, a present trigger, a concrete consequence, behaviour with contextual support, or delayed disclosure where comprehension remains intact.|
|Meaning preservation risk|A hint may be too vague; a rewritten history may create unsupported causality or alter Canon.|
|Anti-mechanical warning|Do not force every past fact into a prop or flashback. Retain direct information when it is the active relational action and authorised by Canon.|

## E. Abstract Intent Summary

**Example:** “她决定不再相信他。”

|Question|Draft answer|
|---|---|
|Why it can be weakly shootable|It names a decision but does not show what the decision changes now, or whether the decision is locked story input.|
|Lawful conditions|A Showrunner-provided decision in a scene brief, an authorised narration/interior device, or direct dialogue whose declaration itself changes the relation.|
|When to transform|When a scene-action line merely asserts the decision without an observable commitment, refusal, altered plan, or consequence.|
|Conversion channels|Refusal, revoked access, changed plan, conditional demand, action that commits to a new course, explicit declaration, or a choice under pressure.|
|Meaning preservation risk|Changing distrust into confrontation, confession, or a new plan can change major causality or arc direction.|
|Anti-mechanical warning|Scene Writer may stage an approved decision; it may not replace it with a “more natural” alternative that changes Showrunner intent.|

## F. Weak Causal Summary

**Example:** “因此他终于理解了一切。”

|Question|Draft answer|
|---|---|
|Why it can be weakly shootable|The causal link and conclusion are compressed into an inaccessible assertion; the audience cannot assess what evidence or action changed the state.|
|Lawful conditions|Authorised narration, a concise approved transition that does not pretend to be scene action, or dialogue that intentionally declares the conclusion.|
|When to transform|When the line claims a visible scene outcome but omits the causal stimulus, discovery, choice, or changed behaviour.|
|Conversion channels|Relevant discovery, changed response, revised choice, new question, line, action consequence, or a clue that changes the audience’s working inference.|
|Meaning preservation risk|Making the causality explicit can over-explain, turn uncertainty into certainty, or invent a revelation absent from Canon.|
|Anti-mechanical warning|No reveal is required. A valid scene may end in a choice, a changed pressure, or an unresolved but playable inference.|

## Boundary Notes

- No category by itself authorises deletion, replacement, or a new plot fact.
- No category gives final camera/lens/coverage, final staging, or acting-microdetail authority.
- Shared QA retains language and voice checking; this taxonomy diagnoses **scene legibility and dramatic carrier**, not wording quality.
- The evidence base does not support a universal narration policy. Approved narration/voice-over remains a project-level authority question.
